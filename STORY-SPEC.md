## 1 · IDENTITY
- **Working title:** *The Year She Met Herself*
- **Category / genre:** Adult literary / upmarket book-club fiction, with a controlled speculative (literary-fabulist) spine and a quiet psychological-thriller undertow.
- **Comps ("X meets Y"):** *The Midnight Library* (Matt Haig) **meets** *Dark Matter* (Blake Crouch) — carried on the intimate relational ache of *Normal People* (Sally Rooney) and the domestic precision of Maggie O'Farrell.
- **Logline (ONE sentence):** On the morning of her thirty-fourth birthday, a sensible, much-praised Bristol architect is cursed with a year in which every major choice shows her — in brutal, lived detail — the life she'd have had if she'd chosen otherwise, and she must learn to choose one life fully before the most impressive version of herself overwrites the one she's actually living.
- **Audience / shelf:** Readers 30–55, predominantly women, who buy upmarket book-club fiction; reading-group and "what would I have done" debate appeal. The genre promise we must keep: an emotionally devastating but not nihilistic interrogation of career-vs-love-vs-family-vs-freedom, with a heroine whose choices feel like the reader's own.

## 2 · ENGINE  *(skill: structure-storyteller)*
- **Central dramatic question:** Will Eleanor stop living the life that keeps everyone else comfortable and choose the one life that is actually hers — even with no guarantee it will make her happy — before a more certain version of herself takes her place?
- **Theme as ARGUMENT (not topic):** *You cannot keep your life safe by refusing to choose. Keeping every door open is not freedom; it is the one choice that quietly costs you your whole life. The only happiness that lasts is the one you pick fully and without apology — knowing, and grieving, everything you give up to have it.* (Topic would be "regret" or "the road not taken." The book argues something sharper and almost anti-*Midnight-Library*: meaning comes not from sampling lives but from **renunciation** — choose, lose, and be real.)
- **The conflict engine ("the catch"):** The visions are not neutral windows. Each is a complete, seductive, *finished* life, and the more Eleanor visits one, the more autonomous and hungry that self becomes. The most certain version of her — Ellis, who took the career she always wanted — begins to bleed into the real timeline and overwrite Eleanor's days. Refusing to choose doesn't protect her; it leaves her **blank**, and a blank self can be written over. The pressure that drives the whole book: *you can only be erased if you have not yet chosen who you are.*
- **Movements / acts (5):**
  1. **ONSET (Autumn).** Eleanor is about to accept the safe shape of her life (Adam's proposal, the safe firm, the slow care of her dementing mother) — **but** on her birthday the glimpses begin, and the first one cracks the certainty she built everything on; **therefore** she can no longer pretend the doors are closed.
  2. **TEMPTATION (Winter).** **Because** the visions show her four whole lives, she starts testing them, leaning especially on Ellis's certainty as her mother's mind goes — **but** the more she uses the visions to avoid choosing, the more the alternate selves wake up, and Ellis begins to bleed through.
  3. **THE TURN / MIDPOINT (late Winter → Spring).** Theo, the love she gave up, returns to Bristol and the love-life becomes reachable in reality, not just in vision — **therefore** Eleanor realises the visions *want* things and one of them is trying to take over: the question flips from "which life is best?" to "who gets to be me?"
  4. **CRISIS (Summer).** **Because** she still won't choose, Ellis advances — Eleanor loses time, makes choices she didn't make, nearly destroys the present life by accident; her father (Freedom's true cost) returns, the child-vision peaks, and her mother, in dementia, hands her the unguarded truth before dying and leaving her a derelict house — rock bottom: **not choosing has nearly cost her every life, including her own.**
  5. **CHOICE (Autumn).** On the last day she is offered the three doors — keep the numb present, merge cleanly into Ellis, or walk away from every ready-made life and start over with no guarantees — **and** she takes the smallest, most dangerous one: she renounces all four finished lives *and* the numb present, ends things honestly, claims the broken house, and begins to build one true life by hand. The visions stop. There is nothing left to show.

## 3 · VOICE  *(skill: prose-craftsman — sacred; locked before drafting)*
- **POV structure:** Single **close third on Eleanor**, throughout — **except** that during a full glimpse the narration migrates *into the alternate self's* close third (still "she," but the interiority, the diction, and the **name** shift to Ellis / Leni / Elle / the mother-self), then snaps back. The reader feels the pull and, later, the threat of not snapping back. Deliberate, controlled, rare — a device, not a habit.
- **Tense:** **Real timeline = past tense.** **Visions = present tense.** The tense-shift is the clean, unmissable signal that we have crossed into an unlived life; it also makes the alternates feel more immediate, more alive, than Eleanor's actual days — which is the danger.
- **Register / diction:** Upmarket literary: lyrical but controlled, image-led, interiority-heavy, undercut with dry English wit so the ache never tips into the maudlin. Metaphor reached from Eleanor's two inherited vocabularies — **architecture** (structure, load, light, foundations, the value-engineered design) and her mother's **music** (intervals, rests, the piece the hands remember after the mind forgets). Concrete domestic and architectural nouns over abstraction.
- **Rhythm signature:** Long, accumulating observational sentences that gather domestic and built detail, punctuated by short flat declaratives that land like a closed door. (Codec: `mxd` — `lng-obsrvtnl` builds, `shrt-shp` lands.)
- **Per-POV voice notes:**
  - **Eleanor "Nell" (present, past tense):** the undecided one. Watchful, accommodating, dryly funny at her own expense, hard for others to read because she has made herself easy to read in only the comfortable register. Her mind reaches for buildings and for the half-second before a yes. Few first-person certainties; lots of noticing.
  - **Ellis (career self, present tense, cold):** imperative mood, clipped, never kept waiting, corrects other people's lines by reflex; metaphors of salt, steel, glass, edges, things gone usefully hard. Certainty as a kind of frost.
  - **Leni (love self, present tense, warm):** loose, sensory, reflexively says "we," laughs too easily, body held often; metaphors of flour, paint, weather, the unremarkable choreography of two people who keep choosing.
  - **Elle (freedom self, present tense, restless):** can't sit still, never says "home," travels light; metaphors of birds that don't land, water, departures-board clatter, sun-damage.
  - **The mother-self (family, present tense, divided):** exhausted-radiant, attention always halved, time measured in a child's needs; the only self who is never alone and never rested.
- **Calibration lines (very-them samples):**
  - **Nell:** *She had always been good at this — the small kindness that cost her nothing and kept the room comfortable, the yes that arrived a half-second before anyone had finished asking, so that no one ever had to watch her decide.*
  - **Ellis:** *Ellis does not keep people waiting and she is never kept. She crosses the wet concourse without looking up, because looking up is what people do when they aren't sure they're wanted, and Ellis has cured herself of that the way you cure meat — with salt, and time, and the willingness to let a part of yourself go hard.*
  - **Leni:** *Leni burns the toast and laughs and scrapes it over the sink, and Theo's hand finds the small of her back without either of them deciding it should, and this — the unremarkable choreography of two people who chose each other and kept choosing — is the wealth she traded the other life to have.*
  - **Elle:** *Elle has never once said the word home and meant a place. She means the ninety seconds before a train leaves, when everything she owns is on her back and no one on earth knows which carriage she's in.*

## 4 · PROTAGONISTS

› **Protagonist 1 — the only true protagonist.**
- **Name (and how it's spelled/shortened):** **Eleanor Hale.** Called **Nell** by almost everyone; **Eleanor** by her mother when it's serious; **El** by Theo. Her alternate selves go by other shortenings of the same name — **Ellis** (career), **Leni** (love), **Elle** (freedom) — and the family-self is, pointedly, still just *Nell, with a daughter*: the road most nearly taken doesn't even change her name.
- **Age / role:** 34 turning 35 over the book's year. Architect at a mid-tier Bristol practice doing safe commercial work (a supermarket, a value-engineered care home) — competent, respected, quietly disappointed in what she's let her talent become. Sole carer, in practice, for her mother.
- **WANT (external, visible):** To get through the year the way she gets through everything — sensibly: marry Adam, keep the job, manage her mother's decline, keep everyone comfortable — and to make the visions *stop*.
- **NEED (internal, hidden):** To want something out loud, choose it fully, and survive being the kind of person who is no longer easy to be around.
- **WOUND (origin):** Her father, Patrick, left when she was nine, and her mother, Diana, never blamed him — she praised Eleanor instead, endlessly, for being *good*, *no trouble*, *so easy*, *so adaptable*, the daughter who made a hard life easier. Eleanor learned that being wanted was a thing you earned by costing people nothing.
- **The LIE she believes:** *If I am good enough and adaptable enough, no one will leave. Wanting too much is how you lose people. Keeping every door open keeps me safe.*
- **ARC (lie → truth, one line):** From *a life unchosen is a life kept safe* to *a life unchosen is not protected, only unlived — so choose one thing fully, grieve the rest, and be real.*
- **Voice signature / verbal tic / physical anchor:** The yes that arrives a half-second early; hands that, she thinks, "have never decided anything"; she straightens other people's picture frames and, in buildings, instinctively finds the load-bearing wall.

› **Protagonist 2:** *None — single-protagonist novel by design.* The "second leads" are the **four alternate selves** (Ellis / Leni / Elle / the mother-self), tracked as full characters in the bible, and the supporting cast below.

## 5 · SUPPORTING CAST
- **Diana Hale** — Eleanor's mother, 66; former piano teacher; vascular dementia worsening across the year. **Function in theme:** the *source of the Lie* (she rewarded goodness and adaptability) and, as her mind dissolves, the unexpected *oracle of truth* — the woman who, forgetting who Eleanor is supposed to be, finally gives her permission to want. **Mini-arc:** from the approval Eleanor is still trying to earn → to a stranger at a piano who hands her, unknowing, the key. The dementia-music motif: the hands remember the piece the mind has lost.
- **Adam Reyes** — Eleanor's partner of four years, 37, a structural engineer (he literally checks whether other people's designs will stand up); kind, steady, a little safe; about to propose. **Function:** the embodiment of the decent, sensible present life — *not* a villain; the tragedy is that he loves the adaptable Nell and has never met the real one. **Mini-arc:** senses her absence across the year; ultimately they release each other honestly. He is allowed his own dignity and his own anger.
- **Theo Marsh** — the love Eleanor gave up at 28 (he wanted the big, messy, uprooting life; she chose the firm and her mother); now 36, recently divorced, no children, back in Bristol. **Function:** makes the Love path reachable in *reality*, not only in vision — love as a true cost with no guarantee. **Mini-arc:** he is not a fairy-tale rescue; he has his own life and his own unhealed version of their ending; if they begin again it is slowly, on honest terms — or not at all.
- **Priya Nair** — Eleanor's oldest friend, 35, two young children, a marriage that is hard and real. **Function in theme:** the model of *choosing one life fully* — and proof that a chosen life is not an easy one. **Mini-arc:** her marriage nearly breaks during the year; she and Eleanor almost lose the friendship over Eleanor's drifting; both choose to stay, changed.
- **Patrick Hale** — Eleanor's father, 68; left at nine; rootless ever since, currently running a bar on a Greek island, charming and hollow and alone. **Function:** the living, ageing proof of the Freedom life — "leave before you're left" is Eleanor's own lie wearing an older face. **Mini-arc:** returns as Diana declines; reaches an imperfect, honest peace with the daughter he left, then leaves again — but as a chosen, named thing this time, not an abandonment.
- **Dr. Ada Okafor** — neurologist, 50s. **Function:** the rational frame and the book-club ambiguity — offers Eleanor a possible diagnosis for the visions (Eleanor, watching her mother, is terrified she's inheriting it). To accept the explanation is to be let off choosing. **Mini-arc:** professional, humane; leaves the door open both ways.
- **Cora Vance** — Eleanor's ambitious colleague/rival at the firm, 31, who wants the partnership nakedly. **Function:** the real-world face of the Ellis path; a mirror of unapologetic wanting. **Mini-arc:** gets what Eleanor "lost," and it costs her exactly what it cost Ellis.
- **Marcus** *(backstory only, kept off-page)* — the man Eleanor was briefly with at 31; the pregnancy and early miscarriage she has told no one about. Named, not dramatised.

## 6 · SETTING
- **Place(s):** **Bristol** — the harbourside, tidal mud at low water, the steep terraces of Cliftonwood and Totterdown in their sherbet colours, the firm's grey office near Temple Meads, Diana's tall thin house on a hill with a piano too big for the room. The alternate lives pull outward: **London** (Ellis — glass, the South Bank, a flat with a view and no one in it), a **Cornish / coastal** small life (Leni — flour and paint and weather), **abroad / in transit** (Elle — departure boards, a Greek island that is also her father's), and the **derelict house** Diana leaves — *(provisional: a former pilot's / lock-keeper's cottage on the tidal river, or a small redundant chapel)* — which becomes the literal site of the chosen, smaller life.
- **Time period / season span:** Contemporary. One year, **mid-October to mid-October**, structured as a 365-day countdown. Autumn (onset) → Winter (descent) → Spring (the turn) → Summer (crisis) → Autumn (choice) → a short coda the following season.
- **The "two worlds":** Eleanor's *actual* life (past tense, grey, careful, load-bearing) versus the *unlived* lives (present tense, vivid, finished, hungry). The deeper "two worlds": **the impressive** (Ellis, London, what the world rewards) versus **the true** (small, honest, unguaranteed).
- **Sensory texture bank:** tidal river-mud smell at low water; the specific wrong note in Diana's piano (a flat D that was never fixed); damp Bristol stone and woodsmoke; the squeak of architect's tracing paper and the cold of a steel rule; supermarket-fluorescent hum of the firm's safe project; the over-sweet birthday cake no one finished; the smell of a house being opened up after years shut; train-concourse rain and the departures-board clatter. *(research-integrator: verify Bristol geography/tides, dementia presentation, RIBA/architectural-practice detail, miscarriage medical accuracy — handle the last two with dignity and precision.)*

## 7 · MOTIF SYSTEM  *(seed → payoff)*
1. **Doors / the open corridor** — keeping every door open = the lie of freedom. *Seed ch1 (Nell loves a house with all its doors open). Payoff ch12–13 (she closes doors on purpose; a closed door is revealed as the wall that holds the roof up).*
2. **The unbuilt house / load-bearing** — the design value-engineered down to nothing; the life that didn't get built; what actually carries the weight. *Seed ch1–2. Payoff ch12–13 (she builds the small true house by hand).*
3. **Hands** — each self has different hands (Ellis's cold, Leni's paint-stained, Elle's sun-damaged, the mother-self's never-still); Nell's "have never decided anything." *Seed ch1. Payoff ch13 (her hands calloused from chosen work).* Braided with the piano.
4. **The piano / the music that survives the memory** — Diana's piano, the flat D, the dementia-music phenomenon, one recurring piece *(provisional: Satie, Gymnopédie No.1)*. *Seed ch1/4. Payoff ch11 (the last of her mother that remains) and ch13.*
5. **Birds: migration vs. nesting** — swifts that never land (Freedom / the father). *Seed ch4/9. Payoff ch13 (she watches them go and does not).* 
6. **Reflections / dark glass** — the alternate selves first seen in train windows and night glass ("walls that have given up pretending to be walls"). *Seed ch1. Escalate ch6 (Ellis in the mirror). Payoff ch12.*
7. **The 365-day countdown** — each chapter timestamped by day. *Seed ch1 (Day 1). Payoff ch12 (Day 365) / ch13 (Day 366 — the first day after).* 
8. **Temperature** — Ellis is cold-handed; the warm lives are warm; the body keeps the score of which life she's in. *(Braided with Hands.)*
9. **The tide / the harbour** — water that goes all the way out and comes back; the loss that returns as shape. *Seed ch2. Payoff ch13.*
10. **"Easy"** — the word everyone uses for Nell ("she was such an easy child"). *Seed ch1. Subvert ch11 (Diana: "You were never easy. You were just quiet about how hard it was."). Payoff ch12 (she chooses to be difficult, and true).* 
11. **Names / the diminutives** — who is allowed to call her what; Eleanor and all her shortenings. *Seed ch1. Payoff ch12 (she names herself).* 
12. **The cold cup of tea / the meal made for others** — small domestic emblem of self-erasure (she makes the tea, eats last, adapts). *Seed ch1. Payoff ch13 (someone finally makes one for her — or she lets her own go cold, on purpose, for her own reasons).* 

## 8 · B-PLOTS / SUBPLOTS  *(reach length through story, never padding)*
- **B1 — Priya's marriage (theme-rhyme: a chosen life is real, not easy).** Priya, the model of full commitment, nearly loses her marriage across the year; she and Eleanor strain and reconcile. Shows the theme isn't "choose and be happy," it's "choose and be alive." Resolution: she stays, changed and honest (or leaves, honestly — *provisional, decide ch10*). *Advances: ch2, 5, 8, 10, 13.*
- **B2 — The firm / the value-engineered building & Cora (theme-rhyme: career as a kind of happiness that is also a hiding place).** Eleanor's professional fork: the safe partnership track (real-world Ellis) vs. a true design — the restoration of the derelict house, or a small community commission. Cora takes the ambition nakedly. Resolution: Eleanor leaves to start a one-woman practice — the smaller-on-paper professional life. *Advances: ch2, 3, 8, 12, 13.*
- **B3 — Caring for Diana / the father's return (theme-rhyme: Freedom's true cost; the price of being the one who stays).** The logistics and grief of a dementing parent borne alone; Patrick reappears. Resolution: Patrick leaves again, but as an honest, named goodbye; Eleanor stops trying to hold everyone up. *Advances: ch1, 4, 9, 10, 11.*
- **B4 — Theo's own life (theme-rhyme: love as a true cost, no guarantee).** Theo is not a fantasy; he has a life, a near-relationship, his own unhealed version of how they ended. Resolution: ambiguous-honest — if they begin again, slowly and on true terms, after she has chosen herself; not as a rescue. *Advances: ch5, 8, 12, 13.*

## 9 · INFORMATION-STATE SEEDS  *(continuity — the subtle one)*
- **The visions are Eleanor's alone.** No one else witnesses them. She tells almost no one; late, she tries to tell Priya, who hears it as illness. *Rule: no character may act on knowledge of the visions.*
- **The pregnancy & miscarriage at 31** is a secret Eleanor has told *no one* — not Adam, not Priya. Revealed to the **reader** ~ch7; spoken **aloud** by Eleanor only at ch11–12. *Rule: Adam/Priya cannot reference it before she tells them.*
- **The true reason her father left** (and that Diana, not Eleanor, was the one who told him to go — *provisional twist, confirm in ch11*) is unknown to Eleanor until Diana, in dementia, lets it slip in ch11. *Rule: Eleanor operates on the child's false version until then.*
- **Ellis's takeover acts** (the off-page emails, the cut hair, the London application) happen in lost time; the reader and Eleanor discover each *after* the fact. *Rule: log every Ellis-action and the exact chapter Eleanor learns of it.*
- **Theo's full status** (recently divorced; why; whether he's seeing someone) is revealed in stages — ch5 (back in town), ch8 (the divorce), ch12 (the truth of what their ending cost him). *Rule: Eleanor doesn't know until told.*
- **Dr. Okafor's possible diagnosis** (ch6) is never resolved on the page; Eleanor declines further testing (ch10/12). *Rule: keep it genuinely ambiguous — the text honours the visions as real without ever disproving the illness.*
