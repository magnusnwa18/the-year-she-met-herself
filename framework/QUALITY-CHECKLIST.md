# QUALITY CHECKLIST — The Gate
### Nothing advances until it passes. Run the per-scene gate while drafting, the per-chapter gate at each chapter's end, the book gate in revision.

---

## PER-SCENE GATE  *(run as you draft each ◆ scene)*
- [ ] **POV is clear and consistent** within the scene; tagged with the POV character.
- [ ] **Want + obstacle:** the POV character wants something here and something is in the way.
- [ ] **Earns its place (Law 2):** does ≥2 of {advance plot, advance subplot, deepen character, build essential texture}.
- [ ] **Theme rhyme (Law 2):** the scene quietly tests the theme-argument.
- [ ] **Causal link (Law 3):** connects to the previous scene by because/therefore/but, not "and then."
- [ ] **Voice (Law 1):** sounds like the calibration lines, not generic prose.
- [ ] **Continuity (Law 6):** no one uses knowledge they don't have yet; details match the bible.
- [ ] **Craft hygiene:** minimal filtering ("she saw/felt/realized"), strong verbs over adverbs, varied sentence length, dialogue carries subtext (people don't say exactly what they mean), description is one telling detail not a catalogue.
- [ ] **Motif (Law 5):** if a motif appears, it's deliberate and varied — not on autopilot.

## PER-CHAPTER GATE  *(run at each chapter's end, before moving on)*
- [ ] The chapter did the **job** assigned in `OUTLINE.md`.
- [ ] **Escalation:** stakes are higher / the situation has changed by chapter's end.
- [ ] **Opening hook + closing pull:** it starts in motion and ends making you want the next chapter.
- [ ] **Word floor met** — and met with *story* (B-plot scenes), not padding (Law 4).
- [ ] **Each POV character's arc moved** (or was deliberately held).
- [ ] **Bible updated** with any new canon; information-state still consistent (Law 6).
- [ ] **Tracker updated, committed, pushed** (Law 7).
- [ ] At least one motif **seeded or paid off**, tracked in the bible (Law 5).

## BOOK-LEVEL GATE  *(run in Phase 5 — revision)*
- [ ] **Central dramatic question** is posed early and answered at the climax.
- [ ] **Theme-as-argument** is dramatized across the book and *earned* by the ending — not stated.
- [ ] **Every protagonist arc completes:** lie → truth, tested by the plot, paid off.
- [ ] **Every motif** seeded early is paid off; none is overused (flag and thin if so).
- [ ] **Every B-plot resolves** and rhymes with the theme; none competes with or dilutes the A-story.
- [ ] **No dangling setups; no unsupported payoffs.** Every promise to the reader is kept.
- [ ] **Information-state is airtight** across the whole manuscript (the subtle continuity error).
- [ ] **Timeline / geography / ages** consistent end to end.
- [ ] **Voice is consistent** across all chapters and POVs.
- [ ] **Pacing:** no sagging middle; the midpoint does real work; breaths are placed deliberately.
- [ ] **Chapter lengths** are balanced (or deliberately varied for effect).
- [ ] **The ending pays off the opening** (callbacks, motif closure, the promise of the logline kept).

## THE "IS THIS ACTUALLY GOOD?" SMELL TESTS
Quick gut-checks that catch what rubrics miss:
1. **Read a page aloud (describe the cadence).** If the rhythm is flat or every sentence is the same length, it's not in voice yet.
2. **Cover the dialogue tags.** Can you tell who's speaking from diction alone? If not, the voices aren't distinct.
3. **The "so what" test.** For any scene, ask "so what?" three times. If you hit a dead end, the scene lacks stakes.
4. **The motif audit.** Could a reader, at the end, point to one image and feel it accrue meaning across the book? If yes, it's composed.
5. **The cut test.** Could this scene be deleted with no loss? If yes, delete it (Law 2).
6. **The lie test.** For each protagonist: name the lie they believed on page 1 and the truth they hold at the end. If they're the same, the character didn't arc.

If it passes the rubric *and* the smell tests, it's the quality bar this framework exists to reproduce.
